What this is

20,603 distinct narrations from eight collections, filtered to marfūʿ reports — what the Prophet ﷺ said, did, ordered, or approved. Companion opinions and compiler chapter commentary are screened out, near-identical repetitions through different chains are merged and recorded rather than deleted, and the whole corpus is arranged across 67 practical topics instead of the compilers' original chapters.

Read the limits before relying on any of it

Only Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī and Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim are sound throughout — that is what Ṣaḥīḥ in their titles claims. The four Sunan deliberately include weak material, so each narration carries a grade; al-Muwaṭṭaʾ and the partial Musnad Aḥmad have no grading source and show as ungraded, which means unverified, not authentic.

Grades come from the LK Ḥadīth Corpus (Leeds / King Saud University) and are al-Albānī's; other scholars grade differently. They were joined to each narration by matching wording, because the two sources number ḥadīth incompatibly — that join succeeded for over 99% in every collection, and it self-checks, since Bukhārī and Muslim come back essentially all ṣaḥīḥ.

Topic labels are assigned by keyword, so a narration can sit under a topic it only touches in passing. Browse with them; don't cite them. Reference numbers follow the source dataset and differ from sunnah.com's, so each card carries a verify link that searches by wording instead. For anything you intend to act on, read the Arabic and ask someone trained in the sciences of ḥadīth.
